# Simple Past vs Past Perfect

## At a glance

Start aloud: *The bus left at seven. Ina arrived at five past seven.* Draw two dots on the board. Ask *“Ina masih bisa naik bus itu?”* Let a learner point to the first event and answer in Indonesian or English. Then say *“When Ina arrived, the bus had left.”* Invite the learner to try again with *train*.

**Core idea:** Simple Past tells us about a completed event in the past. Past Perfect tells us that an event happened before a later point or event in the past.

**Example:** *When I arrived, the train had left.* — Ketika saya tiba, kereta sudah berangkat.

- **Later past event:** I arrived.
- **Earlier past event:** the train had left.

**Audience:** Grades 10–12, with mixed proficiency. Use the supported entry route for learners who need to rebuild basic verb knowledge. Grade does not prove proficiency.

**Teaching resources:** teacher's phone and whiteboard. No worksheet, slide deck, or external audio is required.

**Important:** Past Perfect is not simply “a more distant past”. It marks an event as earlier than a past reference point. If the order is already clear, Simple Past can often describe both events.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the topic, students should be able to:

1. Identify the earlier and later events in a past situation.
2. Recognise and form Simple Past (`V2`) and Past Perfect (`had + V3`).
3. Choose a tense according to the intended timeline and context.
4. Correct common errors involving `did`, `had`, V2, and V3.
5. Describe a short past experience using both tenses where the timeline calls for them.

## Prerequisites

Students benefit from knowing:

- A basic English sentence pattern: subject + verb + (object/complement).
- The idea of past time.
- A small set of common verbs in base form and Simple Past.

Do not block the lesson if students do not know every irregular verb. Teach the needed forms as they appear.

**Quick prerequisite check:** Ask students to give the past form of *go*, *see*, and *finish*. Expected: *went*, *saw*, *finished*. If they cannot yet do this, use the remedial support section before asking them to produce Past Perfect independently.

## Teaching path

| Stage | Student action | Support |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Try the two models | Describe a finished event, then look back from a later moment. | Ten choices per pattern; select prompts to suit the group. |
| 2. See the timeline | Identify which event happened first and which happened later. | Indonesian situation, timeline, numbered events. |
| 3. Build the forms | Compare positive, negative, and question forms. | Formula cards, V2/V3 bank, worked examples. |
| 4. Choose and correct | Select the tense from context and repair common errors. | Context clues first, then fewer prompts. |
| 5. Tell the story | Combine both tenses in a short, coherent account. | Event cards and sentence frames, then independent writing/speaking. |

## Teaching stages

### Simple Past — tell what happened

**Model** — *Yesterday, Ina bought bread at the market.* Tutup kata *bread*. Pilih *milk* atau *fruit*, lalu ucapkan kalimat lengkap. Waktunya sudah lewat. Untuk kata kerja tindakan dalam kalimat positif, gunakan bentuk lampau, misalnya *buy → bought*.

Select a few starters to say aloud. Items 1–5 supply the past verb. Students who are ready can choose and change a verb in items 6–10.

1. Yesterday, Ina bought ___.
2. Last night, Deni ate ___.
3. On Sunday, the cousins played ___.
4. Yesterday afternoon, we walked to ___.
5. Last Friday, the friends shared ___ at the park.
6. Yesterday, the bus ___ near the market.
7. Last night, Rani ___ a message to her cousin.
8. On Saturday, the neighbours ___ to make the park more welcoming.
9. Tell what the friends did when the rain stopped. Start with “They …” and add a reason.
10. A visitor spent one afternoon in town. Tell two events in the order they happened.

<details>
<summary>Vocabulary choices for 1–10</summary>

1. bread / fruit
2. noodles / rice
3. chess / cards
4. the park / the shop
5. snacks / funny stories
6. stop / break down
7. send / write
8. plant flowers / clean the benches
9. go outside / open the windows / fresh air
10. visit a market / buy a snack / walk home / then

</details>

**Quick-fire.** *Yesterday, Ina bought **bread** at the market.* Change the item. Next, replace **at the market** with a place phrase. Cover the model and tell a different finished event from Ina's day. One possibility is *She took the bus home.* If a learner stalls, offer *buy → bought* and *take → took* and let them choose.

### Past Perfect — look back from a past moment

**Model** — *When Ina reached the stall, the seller had packed the fruit.* Draw two boxes: **packed** first, **reached** second. Bayangkan Ina baru tiba. Buahnya sudah dikemas. Gunakan *had + V3* untuk kejadian yang lebih dulu. Ask: “Did Ina see the seller packing it?” In this model the packing was already complete when she arrived.

Complete the first five by choosing a detail. For the others, make the earlier action fit the later situation. Keep one past moment clearly in view.

1. When Ina arrived, the seller had packed the ___.
2. When the film began, we had found our ___.
3. By the time Dad called, Rani had eaten ___.
4. When the rain started, Deni had put his ___ in his bag.
5. By the time the guests arrived, we had washed the ___.
6. When the bus arrived, Ina had already ___ her ticket.
7. By the time the shop opened, the seller had ___ the prices on the board.
8. The table was ready when the guests arrived because the neighbours had ___.
9. The friends couldn't buy tickets at the door. Use “By the time they arrived, …” to explain what had happened.
10. Imagine the end of a picnic. Describe what a late visitor saw and give an earlier action that explains it.

<details>
<summary>Vocabulary choices for 1–10</summary>

1. apples / oranges
2. seats / tickets
3. rice / a sandwich
4. phone / notebook
5. cups / plates
6. buy → bought / find → found
7. write → written / change → changed
8. set out the plates / put cups beside each plate
9. all the tickets / sell → sold / ticket office / close → closed
10. empty baskets / clean benches / eat → eaten / tidy → tidied

</details>

**Possible completions.** *By the time they arrived, the ticket office had closed.* Or, *By the time they arrived, the staff had sold all the tickets.* Either explains what the friends found. Ask which action happened first.

**Quick-fire.** *When Ina arrived, the seller had packed the **apples**.* Swap one word. Replace **packed the apples** with *weighed the oranges*. Hide the earlier clause and ask what else might already have happened at the stall. Accept a new idea if *had + V3* and the timeline both work. If students struggle, offer *packed* or *weighed* and point to the earlier box.

### Understand the sequence of past events

#### Teacher explanation

Mulai dari situasi yang mudah dibayangkan:

> Ketika saya tiba di stasiun pukul 08.00, kereta sudah berangkat pukul 07.50.

Ada dua kejadian:

1. Kereta berangkat lebih dahulu.
2. Saya tiba kemudian.

Dalam bahasa Inggris:

**When I arrived at the station, the train had already left.**

- `arrived` = kejadian yang lebih kemudian (Simple Past).
- `had already left` = kejadian yang lebih dahulu (Past Perfect).

**Makna utama:** Past Perfect melihat ke belakang dari satu titik di masa lalu. Titik acuannya di sini adalah *I arrived*.

#### Whiteboard plan

Tulis dan gambar garis waktu berikut. Tidak perlu memakai warna; gunakan label EARLIER dan LATER.

```text
PAST ------------------------------------------------ NOW
       07:50                 08:00
         |                     |
     TRAIN LEFT            I ARRIVED
      EARLIER                 LATER

When I arrived, the train had already left.
```

#### Model and contrast

1. *The train left at 7:50. I arrived at 8:00.*
   - Dua Simple Past dapat dipakai karena waktu spesifik sudah menunjukkan urutannya.
2. *When I arrived at 8:00, the train had already left.*
   - Past Perfect menegaskan bahwa kereta sudah berangkat sebelum titik waktu kedatangan.
3. *I arrived at 8:00, and the train left at 8:10.*
   - Kedua kejadian memakai Simple Past; kereta berangkat setelah saya tiba.

Jangan mengajarkan bahwa setiap kalimat dengan dua kejadian masa lalu wajib memakai Past Perfect. Pilihan tense bergantung pada urutan dan fokus makna.

#### Ask the students

- Which event happened first?
- Which event happened later?
- What does `had already left` tell us?
- Does every sentence about two past events need Past Perfect?

Expected: the train left first; I arrived later; the train's departure was already complete at the time of arrival; no, not if the order is already clear.

#### Student task — Put the events in order

For each sentence, name the earlier event and the later event.

1. When we reached the hall, the show had started.
2. I opened the window, then I turned on the fan.
3. By the time Nisa called, I had finished the report.
4. The bus arrived at 6:10. We left home at 6:20.
5. After the rain stopped, the players returned to the field.
6. When Dad came home, we had eaten dinner.

<details>
<summary>Show answers and explanations</summary>

1. Earlier: the show started. Later: we reached the hall. The show was already in progress before our arrival.
2. Earlier: I opened the window. Later: I turned on the fan. The sequence is clear from *then*; Simple Past is natural for both.
3. Earlier: I finished the report. Later: Nisa called. The report was complete before the call.
4. Earlier: the bus arrived. Later: we left home. The times show the order; Simple Past is enough.
5. Earlier: the rain stopped. Later: the players returned. *After* makes the order clear; Simple Past is natural for both.
6. Earlier: we ate dinner. Later: Dad came home. Dinner was already finished when Dad arrived.
</details>

#### Readiness checkpoint

Students are ready to move on when they can identify the two events and explain which happened first in unfamiliar examples. Look for correct reasoning, not just recognition of the word *had*.

#### If students struggle

Return to two short statements: *The class started at 8:00. I arrived at 8:10.* Ask students to number them 1 and 2. Then model: *When I arrived, the class had started.* Keep the focus on the timeline before naming the tense.

### Extend the forms — negatives and questions

#### Teacher explanation

Sekarang hubungkan makna dengan bentuk.

| Tense | Positive form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Past | Subject + V2 | She **left** early. |
| Past Perfect | Subject + **had + V3** | She **had left** before I called. |

`Had` sama untuk semua subject: I, you, he, she, it, we, they.

**Negative forms**

- Simple Past: subject + `did not / didn't + V1` — *She didn't leave.*
- Past Perfect: subject + `had not / hadn't + V3` — *She hadn't left.*

**Questions**

- Simple Past: `Did + subject + V1?` — *Did she leave?*
- Past Perfect: `Had + subject + V3?` — *Had she left before you called?*

**Kunci:** Setelah `did`, gunakan V1. Setelah `had`, gunakan V3.

#### Whiteboard verb bank

| V1 | V2 (Simple Past) | V3 (Past Participle) |
|---|---|---|
| go | went | gone |
| see | saw | seen |
| eat | ate | eaten |
| leave | left | left |
| write | wrote | written |
| take | took | taken |
| do | did | done |
| make | made | made |
| finish | finished | finished |
| arrive | arrived | arrived |

For regular verbs, V2 and V3 usually have the same `-ed` form. Irregular verbs need to be learned in context.

#### Model examples

1. *Rani visited her aunt yesterday.* — Simple Past; `visited` is V2 and *yesterday* gives a finished past time.
2. *Rani had visited her aunt before she moved to Jakarta.* — Past Perfect marks the visit as earlier than the move.
3. *We didn't see the message.* — `didn't + V1`, not *didn't saw*.
4. *We hadn't seen the message before the meeting.* — `hadn't + V3`.
5. *Had they eaten before the film started?* — `Had + subject + V3`.

#### Student task — Complete or transform

Use the verb in brackets. Choose the tense that matches the meaning.

1. They ______ (leave) before we arrived.
2. My work was still unfinished. By the time the teacher asked for it, I ______ (not / finish) it.
3. Ask about an earlier viewing using *had*: ______ you ______ (see) the film before last night?
4. She ______ (call) me yesterday afternoon.
5. We ______ (eat) dinner at 7 p.m. yesterday.
6. By the time I reached the bus stop, the bus ______ (go).

<details>
<summary>Show answers and explanations</summary>

1. **had left** highlights the completed departure. **Left** also works because *before* gives the order.
2. **hadn't finished** — the work was not complete at the past reference point; `hadn't + V3`.
3. **Had / seen** — Past Perfect question: `Had + subject + V3?`.
4. **called** — a completed event at a stated past time; Simple Past.
5. **ate** — a completed event at a stated past time; Simple Past.
6. **had gone** — the bus left before I reached the stop; `gone` is V3 of *go*.
</details>

#### Quick oral correction

Ask students to correct these forms:

- *She didn't went.* → **She didn't go.**
- *They had ate.* → **They had eaten.**
- *Had he finished?* → Correct.
- *I hadn't saw it.* → **I hadn't seen it.**

#### Readiness checkpoint

Students can form a positive, negative, and question in each tense and can explain why the verb after `did` is V1 while the verb after `had` is V3.

#### If students struggle

Use a two-column board: `DID + V1` and `HAD + V3`. Give a verb pair such as `see / seen`; ask students to place it in the correct pattern before completing a full sentence. Practise one form at a time.

### Choose the tense and correct common errors

#### Teacher explanation

Pilih tense berdasarkan **makna dan titik waktu**, bukan hanya berdasarkan satu kata penanda.

- **Simple Past:** kejadian selesai di masa lalu; sering dipakai untuk urutan cerita atau waktu lampau yang jelas.
- **Past Perfect:** kejadian sudah terjadi sebelum titik/kejadian lain di masa lalu.

Words such as *before*, *after*, *already*, and *by the time* can help readers understand the timeline, but they do not automatically force one tense. For example, both verbs can be Simple Past after *after* when the order is already clear.

#### Compare the meaning

- *When I got home, my brother cooked dinner.* — The cooking happened when/after I got home (depending on context).
- *When I got home, my brother had cooked dinner.* — Dinner was ready before I got home.

The second sentence changes the timeline, not merely the level of formality.

#### Student task A — Choose the best form

1. We missed the opening scene. When we got to the cinema, the film (started / had started).
2. I (lost / had lost) my key yesterday morning.
3. Before the guests arrived, Maya (prepared / had prepared) the food.
4. They (visited / had visited) Bandung last weekend.
5. By the time the teacher checked the homework, I (submitted / had submitted) it.
6. After he (locked / had locked) the door, he walked away.

<details>
<summary>Show answers and explanations</summary>

1. **had started** — missing the opening scene tells us the film was already playing.
2. **lost** — *yesterday morning* is a finished past time; no later past reference point is supplied.
3. **had prepared** — the preparation was complete before the guests arrived. *Prepared* is also possible in some contexts because *before* already makes the sequence clear; Past Perfect emphasizes completion before arrival.
4. **visited** — *last weekend* is a finished past time.
5. **had submitted** — submission happened before the teacher's check.
6. **had locked** is a good choice to highlight that the door was already locked before he walked away. **Locked** is also grammatically acceptable because *after* clearly marks the sequence; the emphasis is slightly different.
</details>

#### Student task B — Correct the errors

1. When I had arrived, the bus left. (Intended meaning: the bus left before I arrived.)
2. She had went home before I called.
3. Did you had finished the task before the bell rang?
4. I had seen him yesterday at 8 p.m. (No later past reference point is intended.)
5. He didn't knew the answer.

<details>
<summary>Show answers and explanations</summary>

1. **When I arrived, the bus had left.** The arrival is the later past reference point; the bus departure was earlier.
2. **She had gone home before I called.** V3 of *go* is *gone*.
3. **Had you finished the task before the bell rang?** Do not combine `did` and `had` in this question. Past Perfect question order is `Had + subject + V3`.
4. **I saw him yesterday at 8 p.m.** Use Simple Past for a finished time when there is no later past reference point.
5. **He didn't know the answer.** After `didn't`, use V1.
</details>

#### Readiness checkpoint

Students choose the tense by explaining the timeline and can correct at least the recurring form errors (`did + V1`, `had + V3`). Accept more than one tense when the sentence genuinely permits a different emphasis; ask students to explain the meaning they intend.

#### If students struggle

Remove the verb choices and return to a timeline. Ask: “What is the later past point? What had already happened before it?” Then let students choose the tense. Do not teach signal words as rigid rules.

### Tell a past story independently

#### Teacher explanation

Pada tahap ini siswa menggunakan kedua tense untuk menceritakan beberapa kejadian. Tidak semua kalimat harus memakai Past Perfect. Gunakan Past Perfect hanya ketika ingin menunjukkan kejadian yang sudah terjadi sebelum titik masa lalu lain.

#### Whiteboard event cards

Write these events in mixed order:

- I left home at 6:00.
- The alarm rang at 5:30.
- I woke up at 5:40.
- I had prepared my bag the night before.

Ask students to put them in time order and identify which event was already complete before the morning began.

#### Guided model

*I had prepared my bag the night before. The alarm rang at 5:30, and I woke up ten minutes later. I left home at 6:00.*

The first sentence uses Past Perfect because preparing the bag happened before the morning events. The other events are told in Simple Past sequence.

#### Student task — Build a short recount

Students choose one of these fictional situations:

**A. A missed bus**
- You left home at 7:15.
- The bus left at 7:10.
- You reached the bus stop at 7:20.
- You checked the schedule the night before.

**B. A music evening**
- The doors opened at 6:00.
- The musicians checked the speakers at 5:30.
- The first singer greeted the audience at 6:10.
- The group played two songs.

Write or say 4–6 sentences. Include at least two Simple Past verbs and one Past Perfect verb. Students may use these frames if needed:

- Before ______, I/we had ______.
- When ______ happened, ______ had already ______.
- Then, I/we ______.
- After that, I/we ______.

<details>
<summary>Show sample answers</summary>

**A. A missed bus:** *I had checked the bus schedule the night before. The bus left at 7:10. I left home at 7:15 and reached the bus stop at 7:20. When I arrived, I realized that I had missed it: it had left before I got there.*

**B. A music evening:** *The doors opened at six. The musicians had already checked the speakers. Ten minutes later, the first singer greeted the audience. Then the group played two songs.*

These are examples, not the only acceptable responses. Check whether the timeline is logical and the verb forms match the intended meaning.
</details>

#### Readiness checkpoint

Students can tell or write a short sequence that is understandable, use Simple Past for the main past events, and use Past Perfect accurately for at least one earlier event. Their explanation of the timeline matters more than the number of Past Perfect sentences.

#### If students struggle

Provide four event cards in chronological order and sentence frames. Let students say the story orally before writing it. If verb forms are the main barrier, provide a small V1–V2–V3 bank.

## Quick review

Complete the statements:

1. Simple Past commonly uses ______.
2. Past Perfect uses ______ + ______.
3. After `didn't`, use the ______ form.
4. After `had`, use the ______ form.
5. Past Perfect usually marks an event that happened ______ another past reference point.

<details>
<summary>Show quick review answers</summary>

1. V2. 2. had + V3. 3. base / V1. 4. past participle / V3. 5. before.
</details>

**Exit check:** Ask students to explain the difference between *When I arrived, she left* and *When I arrived, she had left*. Expected: in the first, her leaving is at/after the arrival in the described sequence; in the second, she left before the speaker arrived.

## Remedial support

Use this route when students are not ready for independent Past Perfect production:

1. Rebuild subject + verb sentence patterns with familiar verbs.
2. Practise Simple Past with a small verb bank: go/went, see/saw, eat/ate, finish/finished.
3. Add a timeline with only two events and label EARLIER / LATER.
4. Model `had + V3` with two or three high-frequency verbs: had gone, had seen, had eaten.
5. Keep the student task to matching events and choosing between two complete sentences before asking for original writing.

Do not interpret a low starting point as a lack of ability. Reduce language load and increase modelling while keeping the target meaning visible.

## Enrichment

For students who already control the basic contrast:

- Compare two versions of a short story and explain how the timeline changes.
- Explore Past Perfect with *never*, *already*, *just*, and *by the time* in natural contexts.
- Compare Past Perfect and Past Perfect Continuous only after the basic contrast is secure: *She had studied before the test* (completed earlier action) vs *She had been studying for two hours before the test* (duration/process before a past point).
- Rewrite a recount so that the writer begins at a later past moment and then looks back using Past Perfect.

Do not teach signal words as automatic tense rules. Meaning and context remain decisive.

## Assessment connection

This topic can support sentence-level grammar practice and recount/narrative writing. The Main Guide identifies Simple Past and recount-related work among Phase D (Grades 7–9) practical priorities; it does not establish Past Perfect as a compulsory topic for a particular grade. For Grades 10–12, treat this as a grammar teaching recommendation or extension and check the actual school's learning objectives, ATP/TP, and assessment blueprint before claiming school-specific alignment.

Possible formative evidence:

- Student labels earlier/later events correctly.
- Student forms V2 and `had + V3` accurately.
- Student explains a tense choice using the timeline.
- Student produces a short coherent recount with appropriate tense choices.

A short exit ticket can contain: one tense-choice item, one error-correction item, and one sentence about an earlier past event. Use the result to decide whether to revisit verb forms, timelines, or independent production.
