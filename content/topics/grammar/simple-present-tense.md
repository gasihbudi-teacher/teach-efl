# Simple Present Tense

## Try it aloud

*We play cards after dinner.* — Kami bermain kartu setelah makan malam.

Write **cards** and **chess** on the board. Ask, “What do these friends play after dinner?” Let a learner choose and say the whole sentence. Ask *“Sekali saja atau biasanya?”* The friends are describing a routine. Use a fictional family or group of friends throughout.

Simple Present also describes facts. *Water boils at 100°C at sea level.* The verb agrees with its subject in both uses. By the end, a learner can describe a routine, change who does it and keep the verb in agreement.

## Teaching stages

### I, you, we and they — say what usually happens

**Model** — *They share fruit after practice.* Untuk kebiasaan, pakai kata kerja dasar setelah *I, you, we,* atau *they*. *After practice* memberi tahu kapan kebiasaan itu terjadi.

Choose a few starters. Say each one with a word or phrase from the matching line of the bank, or use your own idea. The blanks in 1–5 need just a word or short chunk.

1. We drink ___ at lunch.
2. They play ___ after dinner.
3. I listen to ___ on the bus.
4. You share ___ with friends on Fridays.
5. We ___ at the park on Sundays.
6. At the market, they ___ before they buy fruit.
7. When the bus is busy, we ___.
8. I ___ after a noisy day because it helps me relax.
9. These neighbours ___ on weekends so everyone can enjoy the garden.
10. Invent a weekly routine for two friends. Start with “They …” and give a reason for their choice.

**Quick-fire.** Start with *They share **fruit** after practice.* Change the word to *snacks*. Next, replace **after practice** with another time phrase. Finally, cover the model. Describe something else the friends regularly do together. One possibility is *They walk home together every Friday.*

**Listen for** a base verb with *they* and a plausible routine. If speaking is hard, keep “They share …” on the board, offer *fruit* or *snacks*, and let the learner point before speaking.

### He, she and it — change who does it

**Model** — *They share fruit after practice. Rani shares fruit after practice.* Kebiasaannya sama. Sekarang pelakunya hanya Rani, jadi *share* menjadi *shares*. Say *shares*, *watches* and *studies* aloud. Write *watch → watches*, *study → studies* and *play → plays* where everyone can see them.

Choose a few starters. The first five already supply the verb ending. In the later items, change the base verb if needed.

1. Rani reads ___ after dinner.
2. Amir drinks ___ in the morning.
3. She plays ___ on Fridays.
4. He watches ___ at weekends.
5. The bus stops near ___ every morning.
6. Each evening, Dina ___ the next day's bus times.
7. After lunch, the shopkeeper ___ the counter.
8. When a visitor looks lost, Rani ___ so the visitor can find the bus stop.
9. On rainy afternoons, Amir ___ because he enjoys a quiet activity indoors.
10. Invent a habit for someone in a story. Give the person's name, what they do and when or why.

3. chess / badminton
4. cartoons / comedy shows
5. the park / the market
6. check / study
7. clean / tidy
8. show the way / draw a map
9. read comics / play chess
10. a neighbour / a musician / practise / teach / every evening

**Try a bank item together.** *Each evening, Dina checks the next day's bus times.* With *study*, say *studies*. Both fit the meaning and the subject.

**Quick-fire.** Start with *Rani reads **comics** after dinner.* Swap *comics* for *stories*. Next, replace **after dinner** with *on the bus*. Hide the model and describe a different person's routine. Listen for the ending even when the time phrase changes.

## Exit check

Say *“They study English on Fridays.”* Change *they* to *my cousin*. Then ask for a new sentence about both cousins doing something together. If a learner says *“My cousin study,”* point to *study → studies*, say both versions, and let them try again.

<details>
<summary>Listen for these differences</summary>

*My cousin studies English on Fridays.* One cousin takes *studies*. A possible second sentence is *My cousins play chess after lunch.* More than one cousin takes the base form *play*. Accept any routine that makes sense and agrees with its subject.

</details>
