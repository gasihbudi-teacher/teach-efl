# Additive Conjunctions & Expressions

## At a glance

[Printable lesson (PDF, 19 pages)](https://teach-efl.pages.dev/lessons/additive-conjunctions.pdf)

**Purpose.** Students practise adding related information accurately and meaningfully. The lesson prioritises immediate use rather than lengthy classification.

**Teaching routine.** Teach one pattern at a time: read the model, explain the function briefly, invite completion of selected starters, then use the matching vocabulary bank as needed. The first five prompts are designed to be approachable for pre-A1 learners with support; the final five gradually demand more independent ideas.

**Quick-fire method.** Use a complete model. Students replace one word, then a phrase, then a whole clause/idea. Preserve the target expression, grammar and logical relationship. Reduce highlighting as independence grows.

**Important.** The banks are idea support, not fixed answer keys. Accept other grammatical, relevant and natural completions. Do not require every student to finish all ten starters.

## Teaching stages

### In addition

Sentence connector. Start a new sentence with a comma after the connector; follow it with a complete clause.

#### Pattern 1: In addition, + subject + verb (+ complement)

Gunakan In addition, di awal kalimat baru untuk menambahkan informasi yang masih berkaitan. Setelahnya harus ada klausa lengkap: subject + verb.

**Model** — Many teenagers enjoy listening to music. In addition, they enjoy watching comedy videos.

**Ten sentence starters** — items 1–5 are the accessible entry range; 6–10 gradually add language or reasoning demand.

1. Many teenagers enjoy listening to music. In addition, they enjoy ___.
2. Some people like watching films. In addition, they like ___.
3. Smartphones help people communicate. In addition, they help people ___.
4. A good friend listens to us. In addition, a good friend ___.
5. Sharing food is a kind gesture. In addition, it ___.
6. Music can make a long trip more enjoyable. In addition, it can ___.
7. Talking calmly can prevent an argument from getting worse. In addition, it can ___.
8. Using a map app helps travellers find places. In addition, it helps them ___.
9. Setting aside money each week can build a useful habit. In addition, it can ___.
10. Small acts of kindness can make someone's day better. In addition, they can ___.

## Quick-fire practice

**Model** — Many teenagers listen to **music** because it **helps them relax**. In addition, **it can improve their mood**.

1. Word replacement: Replace only the highlighted word: **music** → podcasts / audiobooks / songs. Keep the rest of the model unchanged.

2. Phrase replacement: Replace the highlighted phrase: **helps them relax** → gives them a break / makes a journey enjoyable / helps them feel calm. Adjust grammar if needed.

3. Clause/idea replacement: Replace the whole added idea: **it can improve their mood** → it can bring back good memories / help them concentrate. Students generate a relevant idea with less support.

**Feedback checks** — Is the idea related? Is the grammar accurate? Does the expression perform its intended function? Are there other acceptable answers?

## Grammar and answer guidance

- In addition, Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally: as sentence connectors, introduce a complete clause; use a comma after the connector.
- In addition to, Along with: prepositional use takes a noun phrase or -ing form, not a bare verb.
- As well as: keep connected elements parallel; when linking subjects, the verb usually agrees with the first subject.
- Not only ... but also: keep the linked grammatical elements parallel.
- Both ... and: join equal elements; a compound subject takes a plural verb.
- Besides: distinguish sentence-adverb use ('also / another point') from prepositional use ('apart from / in addition to').

## Readiness and support

Students are ready to move on when they can explain what extra information is being added, complete a supported prompt with a relevant idea, and preserve the target pattern in a replacement. If they struggle, return to the model, offer two vocabulary choices, and ask them to choose before producing their own completion. Do not use an arbitrary universal pass mark. The expressions overlap in broad additive meaning but are not interchangeable. Teach each expression's own grammar, placement, punctuation, register and restrictions.
