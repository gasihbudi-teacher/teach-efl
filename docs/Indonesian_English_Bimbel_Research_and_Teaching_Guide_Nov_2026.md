# Indonesian Curriculum, TKA, SAS, Formative Assessment & UTBK

## Research and English Bimbel Teaching Guide for Grades 7--12

**Research reference date: 17 November 2026**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Purpose and scope

This guide consolidates the research and teaching framework for an
English tutoring programme (*bimbel*) serving Indonesian students in
Grades 7--12. It is designed for two uses:

1.  **Education and assessment reference:** understand Kurikulum
    Merdeka, TKA, SAS, formative assessment, and UTBK-SNBT.
2.  **Teaching and mentoring handbook:** plan English instruction,
    practice materials, assessment rubrics, diagnostic tests, and
    examination preparation for SMP and SMA/SMK students.

The programme is divided into two tracks:

-   **SMP / Grades 7--9:** Phase D curriculum, foundational English
    proficiency, school assessments, SAS, and progression towards
    independent reading and writing.
-   **SMA/SMK / Grades 10--12:** Phases E and F, academic English,
    school assessments, TKA English, and UTBK English literacy.

> **Important date note:** This document reflects the research response
> prepared for 17 November 2026. Regulations, examination schedules,
> portals, and sample materials can change. Before using a document for
> formal school planning or advertising an examination-preparation
> course, verify the latest version on the relevant official portal.

Key distinctions for English teachers:

-   TKA and UTBK are different examinations.
-   SAS is not a nationally standardised examination with one universal
    question bank.
-   Formative assessment is not necessarily a written test.
-   English curriculum expectations differ between Grades 7--9 and
    Grades 10--12.
-   In the standard TKA subject structure described in the research,
    English is a senior-secondary subject, not a standard SMP TKA
    subject. SMP students should therefore be taught English for
    curriculum and school-assessment purposes, not marketed as preparing
    for "TKA English SMP".

# Part 2. TKA -- Tes Kemampuan Akademik

## 6. What is TKA?

TKA is a standardised academic assessment administered by the Ministry
of Primary and Secondary Education (Kemendikdasmen). Its purpose is to
provide standardised information about students' academic achievement.

It is different from Asesmen Nasional (AN):

1.  Descriptive text.
2.  Recount text.
3.  Narrative text.
4.  Procedure text.
5.  Analytical exposition.

Contexts include everyday, vocational, and basic academic situations.

### Advanced English TKA

The advanced English elective is distinct from regular TKA English. The
research described it as including:

-   Exposition and discussion texts.
-   Technology and factual issues.
-   Everyday, vocational, and academic contexts.

-   SMA/MA/SMK TKA: 200--800 reporting scale.

academic information and may be used in subsequent admission processes
according to applicable rules.

# Part 3. SAS -- Sumatif Akhir Semester

## 10. Understanding SAS in Kurikulum Merdeka

SAS stands for **Sumatif Akhir Semester**. Schools may use related terms
such as ASAS, PSAS, or PAS.

These generally refer to school-level end-of-semester assessment, but
each school determines its assessment arrangements.

Under Kurikulum Merdeka, summative assessment should be based on
learning objectives and content actually taught. It should not
automatically test every competency in the entire phase.

The revised 2025 *Panduan Pembelajaran dan Asesmen* describes
end-of-semester summative assessment as optional where teachers already
have sufficient evidence of learning achievement. Where additional
evidence is needed, an end-of-semester assessment may be used.

Summative assessment does not have to be a written examination. It can
include:

-   Written tests.
-   Oral examinations.
-   Presentations.
-   Projects.
-   Portfolios.
-   Practical performances.
-   Written products.

Reference: [Panduan Pembelajaran dan Asesmen
2025](https://repositori.kemendikdasmen.go.id/33290/1/Panduan%20Pembelajaran%20Asesmen.pdf).

## 11. Suggested English SAS structure

There is no single national English SAS question bank or universal
blueprint. Schools design assessments based on their curriculum,
learning objectives, teaching materials, and assessment policies.

The following is a **suggested bimbel model**, not a national weighting
requirement.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Assessment component              Suggested weight Skills assessed
  --------------------- ---------------------------- ---------------------
  Vocabulary                                     10% Meaning, synonyms,
                                                     antonyms, contextual
                                                     vocabulary

  Grammar                                        20% Sentence construction
                                                     and language accuracy

  Reading comprehension                          30% Main ideas, details,
                                                     inference, purpose

  Text structure                                 15% Generic structure and
                                                     language features

  Writing                                        15% Sentence and
                                                     paragraph production

  Speaking/listening                             10% Communication and
                                                     comprehension

  **Total**                                 **100%** 
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

Adapt the weighting to the school's kisi-kisi and learning objectives.

Examples of grade-level emphasis:

-   Grade 7: descriptive texts, vocabulary, sentence construction,
    simple conversations.
-   Grade 8: recount texts, past tense, reading comprehension.
-   Grade 9: inference, text analysis, paragraph production.
-   Grade 10: opinions, functional communication, text comprehension.
-   Grades 11--12: analytical exposition, discussion texts, critical
    reading, argumentation.

## 12. What a good kisi-kisi contains

A kisi-kisi is an assessment blueprint connecting learning objectives,
content, indicators, cognitive processes, and questions.

  Bentuk Soal            Multiple choice, multiple-response, essay, etc.

  Kunci Jawaban          Answer key
  Pedoman Penskoran      Scoring guidelines

# Part 4. Tes Formatif -- Formative Assessment

## 13. What is formative assessment?

Formative assessment is an ongoing process of collecting information
about student learning. It helps teachers identify:

1.  What students already understand.
2.  What they have not yet understood.
3.  Repeated mistakes.
4.  What they need to practise.
5.  Whether the teacher should adjust instruction.

The 2025 assessment guide describes formative assessment as a way to
monitor and improve learning, identify difficulties, and provide
feedback. It should not be treated as a high-stakes examination for
report-card grades, promotion, or graduation.

Reference: [Panduan Pembelajaran dan Asesmen
2025](https://repositori.kemendikdasmen.go.id/33290/1/Panduan%20Pembelajaran%20Asesmen.pdf).

# Part 5. UTBK-SNBT English Preparation

## 16. What is UTBK-SNBT?

UTBK is the computer-based examination used in Seleksi Nasional
Berdasarkan Tes (SNBT), a national university admission pathway
organised by the national university admissions system, SNPMB.

For English bimbel, the relevant subtest is **Literasi dalam Bahasa
Inggris**.

Official portal: [SNPMB](https://snpmb.id/).

## 17. UTBK-SNBT 2026 examination structure

The research response used the 2026 structure below. Always confirm the
current official framework before creating a simulation or advertising
exact test specifications.

### A. Tes Potensi Skolastik (TPS)

  Subtest                            Number of questions         Time
  -------------------------------- --------------------- ------------
  Penalaran Induktif                                  10   10 minutes
  Penalaran Deduktif                                  10   10 minutes
  Penalaran Kuantitatif                               10   10 minutes
  Pengetahuan dan Pemahaman Umum                      20   15 minutes
  Pemahaman Bacaan dan Menulis                        20   25 minutes
  Pengetahuan Kuantitatif                             20   20 minutes

### B. Tes Literasi

  Subtest                               Number of questions             Time
  ----------------------------------- --------------------- ----------------
  Literasi dalam Bahasa Indonesia                        30     42.5 minutes
  **Literasi dalam Bahasa Inggris**                  **20**   **20 minutes**
  Penalaran Matematika                                   20     42.5 minutes

**Total: 160 questions in 195 minutes.** The English literacy subtest
has 20 questions in 20 minutes, or approximately one minute per
question.

## 18. What UTBK English measures

UTBK English literacy is not simply a grammar or vocabulary test. It
measures the ability to:

-   Find information.
-   Understand information.
-   Interpret information.
-   Evaluate information.
-   Reflect on information.

The research described three broad contexts:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Context                             Examples
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Saintek                             Chemistry, physics, biology,
                                      scientific developments

  Sosial Humaniora                    Economics, history, sociology,
                                      geography

  Personal                            Personal experiences, everyday
                                      situations, literary appreciation
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

### Essential UTBK English competencies

  Competency             Question types
  ---------------------- ---------------------------------------------------------
  General information    Main idea, title, topic
  Specific information   Explicit details, facts
  Inference              Implied meaning, conclusions
  Vocabulary             Meaning in context, synonyms
  Reference              Pronoun and phrase references
  Text organisation      Paragraph relationships, text structure
  Author's purpose       Writer's intention
  Author's attitude      Tone and perspective
  Paraphrasing           Identifying equivalent meaning
  Evaluation             Judging claims and arguments
  Synthesis              Combining information from different parts of a passage

Grammar supports reading comprehension but should not become the primary
focus of UTBK preparation.

## 20. How source eligibility is judged

A source is eligible for use when its provenance, authority, relevance,
transparency, and intended use are clear. Eligibility does not mean that
every source is equally authoritative.

Use this hierarchy:

1.  Government regulations for legal and policy requirements.
2.  Official CP and subject guides for curriculum expectations.
3.  Pusmendik for TKA frameworks and samples.
4.  SNPMB for UTBK structure and rules.
5.  Cambridge English for international proficiency benchmarks and
    assessment rubrics.
6.  British Council for teaching resources and learner practice.
7.  Actual school documents for school-specific assessment examples.
8.  Commercial providers for supplementary practice, not official
    policy.

## 29. Original sample test: Grade 7

### Reading Comprehension -- Descriptive Text

**My School Garden**

My school has a beautiful garden behind the library. It is not very
large, but it has many colourful flowers and different kinds of plants.

Every morning, some students help the gardener water the plants. There
are also several benches where students can sit and read books during
their break.

My favourite part of the garden is a small pond with colourful fish. I
usually visit the garden with my friends because it is peaceful and
relaxing.

Our teachers encourage us to keep the garden clean and take care of the
plants.

**Questions**

**1. What is the text mainly about?**

A. The school library.\
B. The writer's school garden.\
C. The writer's favourite teacher.\
D. The students' daily activities.

**2. Where is the garden located?**

A. Behind the classroom.\
B. In front of the school gate.\
C. Behind the library.\
D. Next to the canteen.

**3. What do some students do every morning?**

A. Clean the library.\
B. Water the plants.\
C. Feed the fish.\
D. Read books.

**4. Why does the writer like visiting the garden?**

A. It is peaceful and relaxing.\
B. It is very large.\
C. It has a computer room.\
D. It is close to the classroom.

**5. What can we infer from the last paragraph?**

A. Students are not allowed to visit the garden.\
B. Teachers want students to care for the environment.\
C. The gardener is responsible for all school activities.\
D. Students prefer playing football.

### Answer key and explanations

  Question   Answer   Explanation
  ---------- -------- ------------------------------------------------------
  1          B        The passage describes the school garden.
  2          C        Explicitly stated in the first sentence.
  3          B        The second paragraph explains this activity.
  4          A        Explicitly stated in the third paragraph.
  5          B        The teachers encourage environmental responsibility.

**Teaching extension:** Ask students to write five to seven sentences
describing their favourite place at school. This turns a reading
exercise into a writing activity. Assess the paragraph using the writing
rubric.

## 30. Original sample test: Grades 10--12

### Reading Comprehension -- B1-oriented informational text

This is a shortened instructional example, not a full-length official
TKA or UTBK passage.

**Reducing Food Waste at School**

Food waste has become a growing concern in many schools. Every day,
students throw away food that they cannot finish during lunchtime.
Although some schools have introduced separate waste bins, this solution
alone may not be sufficient.

One effective approach is to educate students about responsible
consumption. Schools can encourage students to take only the amount of
food they need and explain the environmental consequences of wasting
food.

Another approach is to monitor food waste regularly. By collecting
information about the amount and types of food discarded, schools can
identify common problems and develop appropriate solutions.

However, successful programmes require cooperation among students,
teachers, cafeteria workers, and school administrators. Without
consistent participation, even well-designed initiatives may fail.

Reducing food waste is not simply about keeping the school clean. It is
also about developing responsible habits that students can practise
throughout their lives.

**Questions**

**1. What is the main purpose of the passage?**

A. To explain why school cafeterias should close.\
B. To discuss ways to reduce food waste at school.\
C. To describe how food is prepared in school cafeterias.\
D. To compare different types of school meals.\
E. To explain why students dislike healthy food.

**2. What can be inferred about separate waste bins?**

A. They are completely ineffective.\
B. They eliminate the need for environmental education.\
C. They are useful but insufficient on their own.\
D. They are only necessary in large schools.\
E. They increase the amount of food wasted.

**3. Why should schools monitor food waste regularly?**

A. To increase cafeteria profits.\
B. To identify patterns and develop appropriate solutions.\
C. To determine which students eat the most.\
D. To replace environmental education.\
E. To reduce the number of teachers.

**4. Which statement best represents the author's perspective?**

A. Food waste is exclusively the responsibility of cafeteria workers.\
B. Schools should focus only on providing more food.\
C. Food waste reduction requires cooperation and consistent
participation.\
D. Students cannot influence environmental problems.\
E. Waste management should be handled outside school.

**5. Which actions are supported by the passage? Select all correct
answers.**

A. Educating students about responsible consumption.\
B. Encouraging students to take appropriate food portions.\

D. Monitoring discarded food.\

### Answer key

  Question   Answer    Tested competency
  ---------- --------- ------------------------------------
  1          B         Main idea / textual understanding

# Part 9. Lesson delivery and teaching methodology

## 34. Use the Genre-Based Approach

The official English subject guide recommends genre-based pedagogy. A
practical sequence uses four stages:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Stage                   Full name               Teaching activity
  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
  1                       Building Knowledge of   Introduce the topic and
                          the Field (BKoF)        activate prior
                                                  knowledge

  2                       Modelling of the Text   Analyse an example text
                          (MoT)                   

  3                       Joint Construction of   Build a text together
                          the Text (JCoT)         

  4                       Independent             Students independently
                          Construction of the     produce their own text
                          Text (ICoT)             
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

### Example: teaching descriptive text

**Stage 1 -- BKoF**

Topic: *My Favourite Place*.

Ask: - What is your favourite place? - Where is it? - What does it look
like? - Why do you like it?

Introduce vocabulary: - Beautiful. - Spacious. - Comfortable. -
Peaceful. - Crowded. - Clean. - Surrounded by.

**Stage 2 -- MoT**

Provide an example descriptive text. Ask students to identify: -
Identification. - Description. - Adjectives. - Simple present tense. -
Specific vocabulary.

**Stage 3 -- JCoT**

Write a descriptive paragraph together. Ask students to contribute
sentences and correct grammar collaboratively.

**Stage 4 -- ICoT**

Students independently write about their favourite place and then
describe it orally.

This is more meaningful than explaining descriptive text for 30 minutes
and immediately giving students 20 multiple-choice questions.

**Week 3 -- Apply:** Students complete more independent tasks and
examination-style questions.

**Week 4 -- Evaluate:** Conduct a mini-assessment, analyse mistakes, and
provide remediation.

Repeat with increasing difficulty. For Grade 12, incorporate regular
timed TKA and UTBK practice.

## 41. Five teaching principles

1.  **Teach understanding before memorisation.** Students should
    understand why a grammatical structure is used, not merely memorise
    its formula.
2.  **Teach English through meaningful contexts.** Use school life,
    local culture, technology, social issues, and everyday situations.
3.  **Develop all language skills.** Do not allow examination
    preparation to eliminate speaking, listening, and writing.
4.  **Teach examination strategies explicitly.** Students should learn
    to identify textual evidence, make inferences, eliminate
    distractors, and manage time.
5.  **Make assessments diagnostic.** Every examination should provide
    information about what students need to improve.

# Final recommendations

A successful English bimbel should have two simultaneous outcomes:
students become better English users, and students become better
examination performers.

For Grades 7--9, establish strong foundations and align instruction with
Phase D and the students' actual school requirements.

For Grades 10--12, develop B1-oriented academic English, maintain the
full school curriculum, and gradually introduce TKA and UTBK literacy
preparation.

Do not let examination preparation become synonymous with memorising
grammar formulas and practising multiple-choice questions. Students who
genuinely understand English texts, communicate ideas, and reason
critically will be better prepared for school examinations and future
academic study.

**Recommended first project:** build a complete Grade 7--12 English
curriculum map, followed by diagnostic tests for all six grades. This
creates a reliable foundation for subsequent lesson plans, worksheets,
rubrics, SAS papers, TKA simulations, and UTBK exercises.

# Appendix. Quick reference checklist for the tutor

## Before teaching

-   [ ] Obtain the student's school syllabus, KSP/ATP/TP, or current SAS
    kisi-kisi where available.
-   [ ] Identify the student's grade and phase.
-   [ ] Conduct a diagnostic assessment.
-   [ ] Identify strengths and priority gaps.
-   [ ] Set measurable lesson objectives.

## During teaching

-   [ ] Connect the topic to a meaningful context.
-   [ ] Model the target language or text.
-   [ ] Provide guided practice.
-   [ ] Include independent application.
-   [ ] Integrate relevant language skills.
-   [ ] Give actionable feedback.

## After teaching

-   [ ] Use an exit ticket or short formative check.
-   [ ] Record recurring errors.
-   [ ] Assign targeted practice.
-   [ ] Plan remediation or enrichment.
-   [ ] Update the student's progress record.

## Before creating an examination paper

-   [ ] Confirm the applicable CP and learning objectives.
-   [ ] Check what the school actually taught.
-   [ ] Prepare the kisi-kisi.
-   [ ] Balance textual, inferential, and evaluative questions where
    appropriate.
-   [ ] Prepare answer keys and scoring guidance.
-   [ ] Review question clarity, difficulty, and alignment.
-   [ ] For TKA/UTBK simulations, verify the current official framework
    and timing.
